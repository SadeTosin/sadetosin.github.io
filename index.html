My story

From asking better questions to building the models that answer them

I did not arrive in data through a straight line. I arrived through curiosity, and a stubborn belief that a model is only as good as the question behind it and the person it affects.

I studied computer science and found my way into analytics by asking questions nobody around me was asking of the data we already had. That habit became a career. Over the past five years I have moved from analysis to machine learning, from reporting to production, and from working alone to building things that other people depend on.

Today I am a Data Scientist at a UK fintech, where I build machine-learning models that help banks and insurers manage risk across millions of records. Models I have built have prevented more than £5 million in losses. This sits where accuracy meets consequence: a false positive is a real customer blocked; a false negative is a real loss. That tension is why I write about fairness, explainability and the limits of accuracy as a metric.

Outside work I mentor people who are starting or breaking into data from non-tech sectors, through ADPList, the Black Data Professional Network and Utiva. I know what it is like to build a career without a map, so I spend a lot of my time drawing one for other people.

Speaker bio

Fola Oluwatosin is a Data Scientist at a UK fintech, where she builds machine-learning models that manage risk for banks and insurers across millions of records. Models she has built have prevented more than £5 million in losses. Her work focuses on where accuracy meets consequence: stopping fraud without blocking honest customers. Fola writes on AI fairness, graph neural networks, quantum machine learning and generative AI for Techeconomy, Technext and RaveNews, and was profiled by Zikoko for her work helping aspiring professionals break into tech. She is a Top 10 mentor on ADPList, a Data Science Coach at Utiva and a mentor with the Black Data Professional Network, and she has spoken at Coding Black Females (UK) Lightning Talks and on The UK Tech Table panel by TechBridge.
